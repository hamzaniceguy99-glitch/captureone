How we work

A small operator on the Belgian coast

Capture One is a company incorporated under Belgian law, registered with the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises under enterprise number 1007.807.135, based at Langestraat in Ostend and formed in March 2024. The registered activity is holiday cottages, holiday homes and holiday apartments. Johan Bauwens is the director and the person charged with day-to-day management.

The company is new, and a two-year-old business should not write as though it had a decade behind it. What can be judged instead is how it is structured. Every unit offered has been notified to the regional tourism authority before being listed, holds a fire safety certificate from the municipality, shows its registration number in the listing as the European short-term rental rules now require, and has had its co-ownership documents read — because in an apartment building those documents can prohibit short stays altogether, whatever the owner would prefer. Guests contract with the company itself, as operator. Owners who want their property put to work lease it to the company for a fixed rent rather than appointing it as an agent, because acting for an owner on commission is a regulated activity in Belgium and the company is not registered for it.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

Why does it matter whose name is on the contract?

Because it decides which law applies. Letting accommodation in your own name, as the operator, requires no professional authorisation: you are the counterparty, you carry the obligations, and the guest knows who is responsible. Managing or letting a property that belongs to someone else, for reward and as a regular business, is a regulated activity in Belgium under the law of 11 February 2013, reserved to those registered with the professional institute for estate agents, with protected titles attached. We are not registered, so we do not do it. When an owner wants their flat operated, they lease it to us and we become the operator.

I own a flat on the coast. Can I let it for short stays?

Possibly not, and that is the first thing to check rather than the last. In a building in co-ownership, the deed of co-ownership and the co-ownership rules may restrict or prohibit short-term letting, and a decision of the general meeting can be enforced against you. Owners regularly list a flat first and read those documents afterwards, which is an expensive order to do it in. Send us the deed and the rules and we will tell you what they say, including when the answer is that this one cannot be done.

What does a unit need before it can be offered in Flanders?

Three things, in this order. It must be notified to Toerisme Vlaanderen before it is offered anywhere. It must meet the basic requirements set by the 2016 tourist accommodation decree, fire safety among them. And it needs a fire safety certificate issued by the mayor of the municipality. Since 20 May 2026 the European short-term rental regulation also applies: each unit has a unique registration number which must appear in every listing, and the platforms are obliged to check it. A listing without one is a listing waiting to be taken down.

You are a new company. What happens to my money if you stop trading?

A fair question and the reason this answer exists. For a direct booking with us, the balance is payable on arrival rather than months in advance, which limits what you have at risk to a deposit rather than a holiday. If you book through a platform, that platform's payment protection and cancellation terms apply and they are generally stronger than anything a small operator can offer, so we will not talk you out of using one. We would rather answer this plainly than be asked it later.

Is the tourist tax included in the price?

No, and it is shown separately on purpose. The accommodation price is the total payable for the accommodation itself; the municipal tourist tax is a charge the municipality levies per person and per night, and it is stated as its own line so you can see what is ours and what is not. We would rather show two honest numbers than one number that quietly includes a tax and invites you to think the accommodation costs more than it does.

Can you manage my flat and take a percentage?

No. That arrangement — acting for an owner, letting their property, taking a commission — is exactly the regulated activity we are not registered for. What we can do is lease your flat and operate it ourselves: you receive rent, we deal with the guests, and the obligations of an operator sit with us, including notification, fire safety and the registration number. If you would rather have a commission-based manager, use someone registered with the professional institute, and check the register before you sign.
